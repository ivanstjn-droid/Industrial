# Knowledge base: exothermic reactors and cooling water loops

## Reactor temperature rising while cooling water flow increases
If the reactor temperature rises despite an open cooling water valve, or the controller clearly increases cooling water flow, cooling capacity is usually reduced: higher cooling water inlet temperature (cooling tower, dry cooler, summer operation), fouling in the heat exchanger, or a sticking valve. Check: cooling water inlet and outlet temperature, valve position versus flow, temperature difference across the heat exchanger.

## Sticking control valve
Typical pattern: the controller output changes but flow follows with delay or in jumps (stick-slip), and the controlled variable oscillates. Check: position feedback, valve diagnostics in the positioner, packing. Act only after approval by maintenance.

## Condenser cooling water
A higher cooling water inlet temperature at the condenser raises separator temperature and pressure; the controller increases cooling water flow. Common causes: overloaded cooling tower, fan failure, fouling. Energy aspect: higher pumping power in the cooling water loop.

## Feed disturbances
Loss or change of a reactant feed (e.g. A feed, A and C feed) shows first in feed flow, the feed controller output and reactor pressure, then in analyser values. Check: supply line pressure, filters, isolation valves, supplier quality.

## Composition and purge gas
Changes in inert or B component in the feed show up in purge flow and purge gas analysis. The purge valve opens further to remove inerts. Check: analyser calibration, feed specification.
