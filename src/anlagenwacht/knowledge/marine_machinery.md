# Knowledge base: marine machinery, cooling and propulsion efficiency

## Sea-water strainer clogging
Signals: sea-water flow, sea-water pressure after strainer, sea-water pump power.
Pattern: sea-water flow and pressure after the strainer fall; the sea-water outlet temperature of the central cooler rises; the LT 3-way valve opens further to keep LT temperature. Common in shallow or muddy ports, jellyfish or seaweed seasons. Check: differential pressure across the strainer, switch to the standby strainer and clean, check sea chest grids. Low sea-water flow also risks cooler and pump damage.

## Central cooler fouling
Signals: LT 3-way valve position, sea-water outlet temperature central cooler, LT supply temperature.
Pattern: the LT 3-way valve drifts towards fully open over days to weeks at unchanged load and sea temperature; once fully open, LT supply temperature rises. Sea-water flow is normal. Causes: biofouling, scaling or sediment on the sea-water side of the plate cooler. Check: temperature approach across the cooler, pressure drop, schedule cleaning (back-flushing or opening plates) at the next port call. Check the anti-fouling (MGPS) system.

## Stuck LT 3-way thermostatic valve
Signals: LT supply temperature, LT return temperature, lube oil temperature after cooler, valve position constant.
Pattern: valve position stops changing while load or sea temperature changes; LT supply temperature leaves its setpoint (too hot or too cold), followed by lube oil and charge-air temperatures. Check: actuator, positioner, wax element (for self-acting valves), manual override. Too-cold LT can cause condensation in charge air coolers; too-hot raises engine temperatures.

## Hull and propeller fouling
Signals: main engine shaft power, main engine fuel consumption, speed.
Pattern: at the same speed through water and similar weather, shaft power and fuel consumption rise slowly over weeks to months. Cooling heat load rises with power. Check: speed-power curve against the sea-trial or post-dry-dock baseline, ISO 19030 indicators, hull and propeller inspection by diver; consider hull cleaning or propeller polishing. Fuel penalties of 5–15 % are common before cleaning; with the EU ETS every extra tonne of fuel also costs allowances.

## Gas-turbine compressor fouling and turbine degradation
Signals: fuel flow, turbine injection control, HP turbine exit temperature and pressure, compressor outlet air pressure and temperature, gas generator speed, shaft torque.
Pattern: at the same speed, fuel flow and turbine temperatures rise while compressor pressure ratio falls. Compressor fouling is usually recoverable by online or offline compressor washing; turbine degradation (erosion, blade damage) needs inspection and overhaul. Check: compressor wash history, inlet filter differential pressure, borescope inspection.

## EU ETS and FuelEU Maritime
Shipping companies surrender EU allowances for 100 % of CO₂ on voyages within the EU/EEA and at berth, and 50 % on voyages into or out of it (phase-in: 40 % for 2024, 70 % for 2025, 100 % from 2026). FuelEU Maritime sets greenhouse-gas-intensity limits on energy used on board from 2025. Every tonne of avoidable fuel therefore costs fuel price plus allowances.
