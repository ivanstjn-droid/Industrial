# Knowledge base: pumps, valves and energy efficiency

## Excess power at the same hydraulic output
If the motor draws more electrical power than the delivered hydraulic power (pressure × volume flow) explains, efficiency has dropped. Causes: impeller or wear ring wear, bearing damage, imbalance, cavitation, operation far from the best efficiency point. Energy aspect: excess kW × operating hours gives kWh and € per year – relevant for ISO 50001 and efficiency plans under the German Energy Efficiency Act (EnEfG).

## Throttling by a valve
A partially closed valve raises discharge pressure and lowers volume flow; the pump works against higher resistance. Permanent throttle control is energy-inefficient; the alternative is speed control with a variable frequency drive. Check: valve position, pressure upstream and downstream of the valve.

## Cavitation
Pattern: vibration rises, discharge pressure fluctuates, volume flow drops, often at high fluid temperature or low suction pressure. Check: NPSH margin, suction side, filters, fluid temperature. Cavitation destroys impellers – act promptly.

## Imbalance and bearing damage
Pattern: vibration RMS rises while process values stay stable; motor temperature rises slightly. Check: vibration spectrum (1× running speed for imbalance, bearing defect frequencies for bearing damage), alignment, mounting.

## Compressed air and compressors
Specific power (kW per m³/min) is the key KPI. If it rises, likely causes are leaks, excessive network pressure or poor load sharing. Rule of thumb: 1 bar lower network pressure saves roughly 6–8 % energy.
